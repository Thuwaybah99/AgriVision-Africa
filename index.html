01 / THE PROBLEM

A small bird can be
a big problem.

Across sub-Saharan Africa, flocking birds can damage sorghum, millet, rice and wheat. The red-billed quelea is a striking example: individual birds are small, but large flocks can consume huge volumes of grain.

Traditional guarding takes time and labor; deterrents that play the same sound repeatedly can lose effectiveness as birds become accustomed to them.

01
🌾

Harvests under pressure

Crop-raiding birds threaten yields and income. FAO estimates quelea-related losses across Africa at approximately $88.6 million annually.

02

Constant manual vigilance

Human scaring and field watching consume time. A system that responds only when birds are detected could reduce the need for continuous monitoring.

02 / THE CONCEPT

Detect intelligently.
Deter selectively.

Instead of running a loudspeaker continuously, AgriVision Africa proposes listening and looking for a real bird event. The system then decides whether to activate a short, varied deterrent signal, ideally conserving power and reducing unnecessary disturbance.

03 / THE INTELLIGENCE

WHY TWO MODELS INSTEAD OF ONE?

It sees what sound misses.
It hears what vision misses.

A single sensor can fail in ordinary farm conditions. The innovation being tested is not simply adding a second sensor; it is combining two different kinds of evidence to make a more dependable decision.

▣MODEL 01

Vision intelligence

Camera images → bird likelihood

What it learns: bird shapes, silhouettes, movement and visual context from labeled images.

What it adds: physical evidence of birds near the crops, even if they are silent.

Failure modes: darkness, glare, foliage, distance, obstruction and camera contamination.

◖))MODEL 02

Acoustic intelligence

Microphone audio → bird-call likelihood

What it learns: patterns in labeled bird calls, potentially using spectrograms as model input.

What it adds: evidence of calling birds that may be outside the camera’s field of view.

Failure modes: wind, insects, machinery, other species and birds that are not vocalizing.

Why decision-level fusion?

Each lightweight model can make a prediction independently. A fusion rule evaluates both outputs and, where possible, their reliability. For example, an unobstructed image may be trusted more when wind makes audio unreliable. A loud bird call can still matter when the camera view is partially blocked.

Proposed research hypothesis: appropriately calibrated fusion can reduce false triggers and improve robustness compared with either model alone. This must be tested against visual-only and audio-only baselines.

04 / RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

From field signals
to a farm-ready system.

A stepwise research plan grounded in the team's September 2026 presentation, with recommended validation details added to make the evaluation reproducible.

01

Frame the agricultural problem

Identify crop-raiding species, use cases, farm conditions and farmers' constraints. Document current guarding practices and relevant ecological safeguards.

Problem definition
02

Collect and label synchronized data

Gather bird/non-bird images and audio with challenging negatives (wind, machinery, other animals). Pair records by time and location, document labels and consent where relevant.

Dataset design
03

Train independent lightweight models

Develop separate visual and sound classifiers. Optimize model size, inference latency and energy consumption for low-cost edge devices.

Vision + acoustic AI
04

Calibrate and compare fusion strategies

Try confidence-weighted combination and sensible fallback rules. Compare fused predictions with vision-only and audio-only models on the same held-out field data.

Decision fusion
05

Integrate solar-powered deterrence

Connect camera, microphone, edge computer and speaker. Trigger brief, varied, non-harmful sounds only when model confidence and operational conditions justify it.

Prototype integration
06

Evaluate in real farms

Measure precision, recall, F1, false alarms per hour, trigger latency, power use and bird response. Compare crop damage or bird landings against control periods or plots.

Field validation

06 / DESIGN CHALLENGES

Practical by design.
Critical by evaluation.

A good model is not enough. The system has to work at a farm edge, in changing weather, with real birds, restricted budgets and limited power.

False positive rate in windy and noisy conditions

Recall across daylight, dusk and partially hidden birds

Power and connectivity limits

Offline edge inference, solar supply, duty cycling and compact models

Battery life, latency, energy per inference

Bird habituation

Short, varied and context-dependent non-harmful sounds instead of constant playback

Behavior changes over repeated exposure

Affordability and deployment

Real build cost, maintenance, coverage per node

Unintended ecological effects

Limit playback, avoid unsafe sound levels, assess effects on livestock and non-target wildlife

07 / WHAT SUCCESS WOULD LOOK LIKE

Not just more detections.
Better decisions for farmers.

The intended outcomes are more dependable detection, fewer unnecessary alarms, less manual guarding, and ultimately less crop damage. None should be reported as an achieved result until tested.

01 / DETECTIONPrecision · Recall · F1

Compare fused AI against camera-only and microphone-only models.

02 / RESPONSELatency · False alarms

Measure how quickly the system responds and how often it triggers unnecessarily.

03 / FARM EFFECTLandings · Crop damage

Monitor behavior and damage over repeated controlled field trials.

04 / ACCESSIBILITYCost · Energy · Maintenance

Verify suitability for small farms, not only model accuracy in a lab.
